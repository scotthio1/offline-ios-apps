In this frame

    Everything runs on this device; nothing is uploaded. Star list: Gaia DR3 to magnitude 12 (brightest 40 per square degree), plus the naked-eye stars Gaia misses. Objects: Messier, NGC/IC, Sharpless (Siril lists). Arrows point the way the camera must turn, as seen in this photo.

    Polar alignment · 3 photos

    Point anywhere with stars (no need to see the pole). Take a photo, turn the RA axis 20–40°, take another, turn again, take a third. Pick all three here. The app finds the mount's axis and tells you how far to turn the altitude and azimuth knobs. Repeat to check.

      Tracking check · 2 or more photos

      Pick a few of your normal subs from one target, minutes apart (the more time between them, the better). The app measures how fast the stars drift and gives the longest sub before stars trail.

      Exposure advisor · 1 test photo

      Pick one raw test frame of the sky (ARW or DNG). Optionally pick a dark or bias frame with it (select both) to measure the camera noise; for your a6700 it is already known.

      Focus check

      Shoot a short frame, add it here, turn the focus ring a little, shoot and add again. The chart tracks star size (FWHM); the lowest point is the sharpest focus.

      Framing planner

      Camera angle is where the top of your photo points on the sky (0° = north, 90° = east). The orange tick marks the top of the frame. Default sensor is the a6700.

      Sky check · is it really clear?

      Before setting up, take a long exposure of the sky with the iPhone on something steady: the 1× camera in Night mode, as long as it allows (or a raw long exposure from a camera app). Pick it here. The app recognises the stars and marks where they are missing: thin cloud you cannot see by eye. A test frame from the a6700 works too.

      Flats check

      Pick your flat frames (all of them, or 10–20). Optionally pick one or two light frames from the same session: the check then divides them by the flats and measures whether the corners come out even. Everything is measured from the raw numbers.

        Grey: the flat from centre to corner (vignetting). With lights: dashed is the light's sky before, blue after dividing by the flat. Well-matched flats give a flat blue line near 100 % (a target in the centre lifts its left end).
