      Why these lines, and why plasma? 📚

      Sources are cited for direction and rough size. The numbers in this app are model-matched to these results, not measured plasma thresholds.

      • Time asleep (sleep line). Gardiner et al. 2023 (Sleep Medicine Reviews, 24 studies, pooled): caffeine cut total sleep by about 45 min; a 107 mg coffee should be finished at least 8.8 h before bed, and about 217 mg at least 13.2 h. The app's Normal line matches that cutoff in this model (0.8 mg/L).
      • Going to sleep (falling-asleep line). Gardiner et al. 2024 (Sleep, 23 young men, crossover): 100 mg at 4 h before bed had no measurable effect; 400 mg at 4 h lengthened falling asleep by about 14 min and cut sleep by about 51 min. The app's default 1.5 mg/L is the bedtime level 100 mg at 4 h gives in a typical 75 kg man, the lower end of that range.
      • Alertness (alertness line). No peer-reviewed plasma threshold for alertness was found. Kaplan et al. 1997 (J Clin Pharmacol): 250 mg helped psychomotor tests more than 500 mg, and anxiety rose with concentration. The 1.0 mg/L default is a rough low-end value to adjust, not a validated line.
      • Sensitive people. Landolt et al. 1995: 200 mg with a 07:10 coffee still disturbed sleep that night. In this model that is about 0.56 mg/L at 23:00, so the Sensitive line (0.4) sits below it.
      • Plasma or brain? Caffeine crosses into the brain quickly. In an equilibrium PET study (Elmenhorst et al.), plasma and cerebrospinal fluid caffeine were about equal (ratio ≈ 1), and CSF lagged plasma by roughly an hour in animal data. So plasma is a fair stand-in for brain levels at the hours-scale that matters for sleep. Half-maximal adenosine receptor displacement in that PET study needed about 67 µM, roughly 13 mg/L in plasma, which is far above all three lines, so receptor occupancy alone does not explain the sleep effects. The lines are behavioural calibrations.
